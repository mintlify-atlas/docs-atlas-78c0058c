title: Spaced repetition
description: Learn how LC Tracker schedules problem reviews using spaced repetition, what each rating means, and how problems get retired.

Every problem you solve is automatically added to the spaced repetition (SR) queue. LC Tracker schedules your reviews using a spacing algorithm that adjusts intervals based on how well you performed — so you spend more time on problems you struggle with and less on ones you've mastered.

## How the algorithm works

When you finish a problem, you rate your performance on a scale of 1 to 3. That rating determines when you'll see the problem again.

The next review date also accounts for:
- **Difficulty** — Easy problems back off quickly (longer gaps); Hard problems stay closer together
- **Consecutive successes** — the more times in a row you rate 2 or 3, the longer the next interval
- **SR aggressiveness setting** — AGGRESSIVE mode applies a 0.7× multiplier to all intervals, making reviews come back sooner

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What happens when I rate a problem 1?">
    Rating 1 means you struggled. The problem is scheduled to come back in **2 days**, regardless of difficulty. Your `consecutiveSuccesses` counter resets to zero, which compresses future intervals.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What happens when I rate a problem 2?">
    Rating 2 means you solved it but needed hints or effort. The interval depends on how many consecutive successes you have:
    - **First ever solve (0 previous successes)**: 3 days
    - **1st consecutive success**: ~4 days (adjusted for difficulty)
    - **2nd consecutive success**: ~14 days
    - **3rd+ consecutive successes**: ~45 days

    Your `consecutiveSuccesses` count increases by 1 each time you rate ≥ 2.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What happens when I rate a problem 3?">
    Rating 3 means you solved it cleanly and confidently. The interval is extended by 25% on top of the rating 2 interval as a reward. Your `consecutiveThrees` count also increments, which contributes toward eventual retirement.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Retirement

A problem is retired when you've demonstrated lasting mastery. Two conditions must both be true:

1. You've rated the problem ≥ 2 enough times in a row to cross the difficulty threshold:
   - **Easy** — 2 consecutive successes (rating ≥ 2)
   - **Medium** — 4 consecutive successes
   - **Hard** — 6 consecutive successes
2. You've rated the problem 3 at least **2 times total** (`consecutiveThrees ≥ 2`)

Both conditions must be met simultaneously. This prevents retirement from a streak of rating-2 solves alone — you need to have demonstrated confident mastery at least twice.

Retired problems no longer appear in your review queue. They show a solid checkmark in the Problem Library.

## The review queue

Each day, LC Tracker selects the reviews that fit within your time budget. If more problems are due than your budget allows, the queue is prioritized by:
1. Most overdue (furthest past their due date)
2. Weakest performance (lowest last rating)

Deferred reviews remain in the queue and surface again the next day.

## Cold Solve

A Cold Solve is a special review that appears in the Daily Plan when a problem hasn't been touched in 30 or more days. It's treated as a fresh solve — no hints, no review context — to test whether the pattern has stuck in your long-term memory. Cold Solve sessions are logged separately from regular reviews.

## SR aggressiveness setting

You can change how aggressively reviews are scheduled in Settings under **SR Aggressiveness**:

| Setting | Behavior |
|---------|----------|
| **RELAXED** (default) | Standard intervals — good for building sustainable habits |
| **AGGRESSIVE** | All intervals reduced by 30% — higher review volume, faster reinforcement |

<Tip>
  Start with RELAXED if you're new to spaced repetition. Switch to AGGRESSIVE only if your review backlog is consistently low and you want to accelerate before an interview.
</Tip>
